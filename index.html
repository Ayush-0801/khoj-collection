Every saree begins

In Odisha the pattern is tied and dyed into the yarn before a single thread is woven. The weaver holds the whole design in memory, then finds it again on the loom. That search is what khoj means.

The weaves

Six families, each from its own cluster of villages, each with a grammar of motifs you can read like handwriting.

Tie. Dye. Weave.

Bandha is resist-dyeing done to the yarn itself. Nothing is printed; the blur at every motif's edge is the proof.

  1. 01

    Bandha — the tying

    Warp and weft are stretched and bound in tight rubber ties, section by section, to the count of the design. A saree can take several thousand.

  2. 02

    Rangan — the dyeing

    Dipped darkest-last, untied and retied between baths. What the ties protect stays pale; what they miss takes colour.

  3. 03

    Buna — the weaving

    The dyed yarn goes on the loom and the weaver aligns pick against pick until the pattern surfaces. Misalign by two threads and the motif is lost.
